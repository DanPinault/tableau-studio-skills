01
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The network is over target on every metric that measures load.

6 operating signals · 67.2% network utilization · previous 90 days
Utilization sits at 67.2% — 2.2 points above the 65% design target, and climbing.
Service metrics are still excellent: on-time shipping and pick accuracy both hold above 99%. But the two cost signals — dock-to-stock hours and labor hours per load — have both turned upward, the first sign that running this hot is starting to cost something.
The story of this brief is the gap between two truths.
Service held — but the price of holding it is rising. What follows traces that price across the network: where the load concentrates, what it's already costing downstream, and how much runway is left before any of it reaches the customer.
02
WAREHOUSE UTILIZATION

67.2% against a 65% design target.

network average · previous 90 days · target marker at 65%
0%
TARGET 65%
Design capacity is the point past which every extra load competes for the same doors, racks, and people. At 67.2%, the network is drawing down the buffer that absorbs demand spikes and staffing gaps.
Above target isn't failure — it's efficiency with no slack left.
The 65% line is where planners expect to run: full enough to be efficient, empty enough to absorb a bad day. Running 2.2 points over the line network-wide means the whole system is trading its shock absorber for throughput. The question the rest of this brief answers: where is it worst, and what is it already costing.

These are the receipts for running hot.
When a building is too full, inbound trailers wait longer to be put away (dock-to-stock climbs) and every load takes more people to move (labor per load climbs). Both have turned up in the last 30 days.
The scatter is the proof the trend lines only imply.
Plotted against utilization, the facilities line up along a clear upward slope: past target, every extra point of load buys measurably slower put-away. Neither cost signal has reached the customer yet — but they are the leading indicators that will, if utilization keeps climbing. That's what makes them worth acting on now, while they're still just hours on a chart.
05
THE 90-DAY TREND

Flat near target for two months — then a sharp climb.

network utilization · 7-day moving average · previous 90 days
NETWORK UTILIZATION · 7-DAY AVG
The last quarter is where the pattern breaks.
For roughly two months the network wavered right around the 65% target — normal, healthy operation. The recent, sustained climb toward 71% is what turns this from a steady-state readout into a brief that needs action.
The trend, not the level, is the signal.
A single day at 71% is noise; three straight weeks of climb is a direction. Hover any point on the line to read that day's network average — the shape is what matters, and the shape has turned. Left alone, the same slope carries the network into peak season already over its buffer.
06
SERVICE STILL HELD

The customer hasn't felt it yet — that's the window.

on-time shipping & pick accuracy · both above 99%
Both service metrics are still up, and above 99%.
This is the counter-trend that makes the story urgent rather than alarming: the network is absorbing the strain internally, protecting the customer experience.
Absorbing strain isn't the same as having none.
On-time shipping and pick accuracy are both still improving — but they're being held up by the same people and doors that the rising cost signals say are stretched. That buffer is finite. Acting now spends slack the network still has; waiting spends goodwill it would have to win back. Now is the moment to relieve the pressure while service is still a strength, not after it becomes a complaint.
07
RECOMMENDED ACTIONS

Relieve the Midwest, protect the trend.

3 execution moves for the week ahead
1
Open overflow capacity for DC Prime & Crossdock North. Both Midwest sites lead the network on utilization. Route ~10% of inbound to a partner cross-dock this month to pull them back under target before peak.
2
Staff to the dock-to-stock leading indicator, not the backlog. Add put-away labor at the two hottest sites now — while the metric is drifting, before it spikes.
3
Set a weekly utilization-vs-target checkpoint. Watch the 7-day trend, not the daily number. The goal is to bend the climb back toward 65% while on-time and accuracy are still strengths.
