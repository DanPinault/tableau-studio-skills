Where we landed
Nearly half the erosion is revenue we earned but never billed.
The single biggest bite — 1.8 points — isn't a cost at all. It's accessorial, detention, and rate work performed and never invoiced: margin we already produced and gave away. Labor cost creep takes another 1.6, and cost-to-serve 0.6.
Two of these three are recoverable without touching operations.
Unbilled revenue and under-applied rates are billing problems, not capacity problems — they're recovered with an audit and a repricing conversation, not a new building. Only the labor slice traces back to the same congestion the operations brief flagged. That's the good news hiding in the bridge: the largest leak is also the cheapest to close.

Monthly unbilled revenue climbed 75% in a single quarter — $383K to $670K.
This is the compounding cost of the quiet part: every month the audit waits, roughly two-thirds of a million dollars of already-completed work ages further out of easy recovery.
Recoverable is not the same as recovered.
Unbilled revenue has a shelf life — the older it gets, the harder the client conversation and the lower the collection rate. The run-rate isn't a projection of new losses; it's the price of the delay itself. Closing the billing gap now converts a leak back into margin the network already earned.
RECOMMENDED ACTIONS

Recover the billing, reprice the leak, protect the anchor.

3 finance moves for the quarter ahead
1
Launch an unbilled-revenue audit at Crossdock North & DC Prime. Start where the leak is largest — accessorial, detention, and rate true-ups. Recovering even half the $1.4M book is 1+ point of margin back, with no operational change.
2
Reprice Outdoor / Powersports and Automotive contracts. Both sit in the danger quadrant on thin margins and heavy unbilled work. Move to activity-based accessorial billing at renewal so the work that's being done is the work that's invoiced.
3
Protect DTC / Retail as the margin anchor. It's the counter-trend proving the model still works — the highest margin and the lowest leakage in the book. Ring-fence its service levels while the recovery runs.
